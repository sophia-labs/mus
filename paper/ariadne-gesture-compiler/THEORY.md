# Inverse geometry of acoustic control paths

**Status:** theorem-and-experiment packet for Ariadne round 3  
**Branch:** `agent/ariadne-gesture-compiler`  
**Relationship to the preprint:** this is the converse program of *Ariadne: Work and Holonomy in Time-Varying Acoustic Networks*. The preprint maps a path to a receipt. This packet begins mapping a desired receipt or state transformation back to a certified path.

## 1. The inverse problem

Let an authored acoustic graph have an energy-normalized state space of dimension `n`. Its admissible zero-work controls are sparse Givens rotations on graph edges,

\[
R_{ij}(\theta)=\exp(\theta J_{ij}),
\qquad (i,j)\in E(G),
\]

and its admissible work-bearing controls are positive factors

\[
H_k=\exp(S_k), \qquad S_k=S_k^\top.
\]

The forward semantics computes, for a control word,

\[
A_\gamma=U_mH_m\cdots U_1H_1,
\]

its polar receipt, state-dependent work, loss, and monodromy. The inverse problem is:

> Given a target metric-compatible transport, deformation, work budget, duration, and graph, decide reachability and synthesize a factor path with a checkable certificate.

This packet makes five concrete advances:

1. an exact graph-constrained compiler for every target in `SO(n)`;
2. an operator-level characterization of reachable work–holonomy pairs after adding one anisotropic actuator;
3. an exact thermodynamic scheduling law and a graph-search prototype;
4. a closed-control-loop compiler for general `SO(3)` targets;
5. separate geometric and topological mode-bundle prototypes, including lowering to edge Givens schedules.

The restricted-rotation QR theorem is not claimed as new linear algebra. O'Leary and Bullock studied QR and permutation synthesis under restricted Givens graphs. The Ariadne contribution target is the integration of exact graph compilation with work accounting, closed control paths, acoustic metrics, gesture cost, receipts, and an authoring language.

## 2. Reachability of lossless transport

### Definition 1 — edge control graph

Let `G=(V,E)` be a finite undirected graph with `V={0,...,n-1}`. For each edge `(i,j)`, let

\[
J_{ij}=e_je_i^\top-e_ie_j^\top\in\mathfrak{so}(n).
\]

The zero-work control system has piecewise-constant generators drawn from

\[
\mathcal J_G=\{J_{ij}:(i,j)\in E\}.
\]

### Lemma 1 — path brackets create missing planes

For distinct `i,j,k`,

\[
[J_{ij},J_{jk}]=-J_{ik}.
\]

Consequently, a path

\[
i=v_0-v_1-\cdots-v_r=j
\]

allows repeated brackets to generate `J_ij`.

### Theorem 1 — connected graph universality

If `G` is connected, then

\[
\operatorname{Lie}(\mathcal J_G)=\mathfrak{so}(n),
\]

and the group generated by the edge rotations is exactly `SO(n)`.

If the connected components have sizes `n_1,...,n_c`, then

\[
\operatorname{Lie}(\mathcal J_G)
=
\bigoplus_{r=1}^{c}\mathfrak{so}(n_r),
\]

and the reachable group is the corresponding block product

\[
\prod_{r=1}^{c}SO(n_r).
\]

#### Proof sketch

Connectivity plus Lemma 1 produces every elementary skew plane `J_ij`, which is a basis of `so(n)`. A disconnected graph preserves each component subspace, so no bracket crosses the cut. Exact group generation follows either from the connected Lie subgroup theorem or from the constructive factorization below.

The pure-core Lean file `formal/MusFormal/Reachability.lean` kernel-checks the path/bracket propagation and cut obstruction at the graph-theoretic level.

### Theorem 2 — exact tree-QR gesture compiler

Choose any spanning tree `T` of a connected graph `G`. Every target

\[
Q\in SO(n)
\]

can be compiled exactly into

\[
m=\frac{n(n-1)}{2}
\]

Givens rotations, each supported on an edge of `T` and therefore on an authored edge of `G`.

#### Constructive algorithm

1. Choose a leaf-elimination order `v_1,...,v_n` of `T`.
2. At stage `k`, retain the tree induced by the surviving vertices.
3. Treat `v_k` as the root. Remove all other leaves toward that root. Each removed leaf uses one edge rotation to zero its entry in target column `v_k`.
4. The column becomes `+e_{v_k}`. Orthogonality fixes the corresponding row.
5. Remove `v_k` and continue on the smaller tree.
6. Reverse and invert the elimination rotations to obtain the synthesis word.

The gate count is

\[
(n-1)+(n-2)+\cdots+1=m.
\]

No routing swaps are required. A path graph, star, balanced tree, or arbitrary connected graph all have the same exact gate count; they differ in angle distribution, actuator weights, parallelism, and robustness.

### Corollary 2.1 — generic scalar-gate optimality

For a finite edge alphabet, a word of `k` scalar rotation angles has image dimension at most `k`. Since

\[
\dim SO(n)=m,
\]

the union of all edge words with `k<m` has Haar measure zero. Therefore almost every target requires at least `m` scalar edge rotations. The tree compiler is generically gate-count optimal.

This is not a lower bound for every special target: identity, single-plane rotations, and sparse permutations can require fewer gates.

### Numerical receipt

The reference compiler tested 360 random connected graphs and random targets for `2 <= n <= 16`. Every schedule used exactly `n(n-1)/2` authored-edge gates. The worst reconstruction error was

\[
2.19\times 10^{-15}
\]

in Frobenius norm. Lie-closure ranks matched `so(n)` on path graphs through `n=8`, and 24 disconnected trials matched the direct-sum component formula.

## 3. Reachability of work and holonomy together

The preprint decomposes a whitened state transport as

\[
A=UH,
\qquad U\in SO(n),\quad H\in\operatorname{SPD}(n).
\]

The question is whether authored controls can reach pairs `(U,H)`, not merely `U`.

### Theorem 3 — one reversible anisotropic actuator gives `SL(n,R)`

Assume:

1. the edge graph is connected;
2. signed edge-rotation controls are available;
3. one signed, reversible, nonzero traceless symmetric generator `S` is available.

Then, for `n >= 2`,

\[
\operatorname{Lie}\bigl(\mathfrak{so}(n)\cup\{S\}\bigr)
=
\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb R).
\]

#### Reason

The Cartan decomposition is

\[
\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb R)
=
\mathfrak{so}(n)\oplus\operatorname{Sym}_0(n).
\]

The traceless symmetric matrices form an irreducible `SO(n)` module under conjugation. A Lie algebra containing all of `so(n)` and any nonzero `S` in `Sym_0(n)` must therefore contain the entire symmetric traceless subspace. Brackets of symmetric matrices recover skew directions, closing `sl(n,R)`.

Under the ordinary driftless, reversible control hypotheses, the reachable connected group is `SL(n,R)`.

### Corollary 3.1 — exact operator-level work–holonomy reachability

The polar decomposition gives a diffeomorphism

\[
SL(n,\mathbb R)
\cong
SO(n)\times \operatorname{SPD}_1(n),
\]

where

\[
\operatorname{SPD}_1(n)=\{H\succ0:\det H=1\}.
\]

Thus one reversible anisotropic actuator plus a connected edge graph can reach every pair

\[
(U,H)\in SO(n)\times\operatorname{SPD}_1(n).
\]

The resulting scalar work remains state-dependent:

\[
W(x)=\frac12x^\top(H^2-I)x.
\]

### Corollary 3.2 — adding an isotropic actuator

Adding the scalar generator `I` expands the Lie algebra to `gl(n,R)` and the reachable connected group to

\[
GL^+(n,\mathbb R),
\]

the positive-determinant component. Equivalently, every pair

\[
(U,H)\in SO(n)\times\operatorname{SPD}(n)
\]

is reachable. A reflection or another discrete operation is required to enter the negative-determinant component.

### Scope and caveats

This is an operator-level controllability result. It does not assert that every transport is cheap, passive, physically interpretable, perceptually useful, or reachable under one-sided actuator bounds. If a deformation control cannot reverse sign, the reachable object is a semigroup and the Lie-algebra statement is insufficient.

The float64 Lie-closure oracle reached rank `n^2-1` with one traceless anisotropy and `n^2` after adding the scalar generator for every path graph tested from `n=2` through `n=8`.

## 4. Thermodynamic geometry of gesture

### Definition 2 — friction metric

For controls `u_e(t)` on graph edges, choose a positive friction metric `Gamma(q)` and define

\[
D[\gamma]
=
\int_0^\tau u(t)^\top\Gamma(q(t))u(t)\,dt,
\]

\[
L[\gamma]
=
\int_0^\tau
\sqrt{u(t)^\top\Gamma(q(t))u(t)}\,dt.
\]

`D` is quadratic effort, dissipation surrogate, or artifact budget. `L` is thermodynamic or sub-Riemannian gesture length.

### Theorem 4 — constant-speed lower bound

For every duration-`tau` path,

\[
D[\gamma]\geq\frac{L[\gamma]^2}{\tau},
\]

with equality exactly under constant thermodynamic speed almost everywhere.

For a fixed discrete gate word with angles `theta_k`, positive edge weights `w_k`, and segment durations `tau_k`,

\[
D=\sum_k\frac{w_k\theta_k^2}{\tau_k},
\qquad
L=\sum_k\sqrt{w_k}|\theta_k|.
\]

The exact optimal time allocation is

\[
\tau_k
=
\tau\,
\frac{\sqrt{w_k}|\theta_k|}{L},
\]

and the minimum is `L^2/tau`.

### Definition 3 — graph gesture distance

The intrinsic cost of a target is

\[
d_{G,\Gamma}(I,Q)
=
\inf_{\gamma:I\to Q}L[\gamma].
\]

At fixed duration,

\[
D_{\min}(Q,\tau)
=
\frac{d_{G,\Gamma}(I,Q)^2}{\tau}.
\]

This is the correct optimization problem after reachability. Exact gate count is topology-insensitive for connected graphs; gesture distance is not.

### Weighted tree-search experiment

For one eight-mode graph with eleven edges and heterogeneous log-uniform friction weights, all candidate schedules used exactly 28 gates. Searching 192 spanning-tree and leaf-order choices changed the minimum quadratic cost from

\[
1480.93\quad\text{to}\quad710.24,
\]

a **52.0% reduction**, while preserving the target to `7.71e-16` Frobenius error. The segment schedule achieved constant thermodynamic speed to machine precision.

This is not yet a global geodesic solver. It establishes that a compiler can make large, certified improvements without changing the graph or target.

### Local curvature cost bound

For a small loop in a two-control chart with approximately constant curvature `F_12`,

\[
\log U_\gamma
=
F_{12}\,A(\gamma)+O(\ell^3).
\]

If the target principal rotation is `phi`, then locally

\[
A(\gamma)\gtrsim\frac{\phi}{\|F_{12}\|}.
\]

For an ordinary planar loop, the isoperimetric inequality gives

\[
L^2\geq4\pi A,
\]

and therefore the small-loop bound

\[
D\gtrsim
\frac{4\pi\phi}{\|F_{12}\|\tau}.
\]

For the rectangular commutator family specifically, `L^2 >= 16A`, giving

\[
D_{\mathrm{rectangle}}
\gtrsim
\frac{16\phi}{\|F_{12}\|\tau}.
\]

These are local asymptotic bounds, not global quantum-speed-limit claims.

## 5. A deformation-budget inequality: what can limit a pump

A cyclic parameter path can pump energy repeatedly. No finite bound follows from endpoint equality alone. A meaningful bound must include an actuator resource.

Let

\[
A_\gamma=U_mH_m\cdots U_1H_1
\]

and define the cumulative logarithmic deformation radius

\[
R(\gamma)=\sum_{k=1}^m\|\log H_k\|_2.
\]

### Theorem 5 — finite-cycle pumping bound

For every nonzero normalized state `z`,

\[
e^{-R}\|z\|
\leq
\|A_\gamma z\|
\leq
 e^R\|z\|,
\]

so the energy ratio obeys

\[
\boxed{
 e^{-2R}
 \leq
 \frac{E(A_\gamma z)}{E(z)}
 \leq
 e^{2R}}
\]

regardless of the interleaved rotations.

This bound is compositional and checkable from local polar receipts. It also shows why a “Carnot bound” cannot depend only on loop topology: repeatable anisotropic deformation permits exponential pumping unless the deformation budget, work port, or dissipation is bounded.

For a twelve-step, seven-dimensional, volume-neutral random sequence, the reference run used `R=1.95459`, predicting the interval `[0.0201,49.86]`. Across 50,000 unit states the observed interval was `[0.292,2.114]`; no violation occurred.

### Theorem 6 — exact factor-order work statistic

For one positive factor `H`, one orthogonal factor `U`, and unit state `x`, define the work difference between the two orders by

\[
\Delta W(x)
=
\frac12x^\top D x,
\qquad
D=H^2-U^\top H^2U.
\]

Then

\[
\operatorname{tr}D=0,
\]

\[
\max_{\|x\|=1}|\Delta W(x)|
=
\frac12\|D\|_2,
\]

and

\[
\|D\|_2
\leq
\lambda_{\max}(H^2)-\lambda_{\min}(H^2).
\]

For a uniformly random unit state in `R^n`,

\[
\mathbb E[\Delta W]=0,
\]

\[
\operatorname{RMS}(\Delta W)^2
=
\frac{\operatorname{tr}(D^2)}{2n(n+2)}.
\]

The 100,000-state reference experiment measured RMS `0.287964` against the analytic value `0.287987`, with zero bound violations. This turns “order affects work” into a complete directional statistic rather than a single witness state.

## 6. Gesture compilation

### 6.1 Exact endpoint compiler

For an unconstrained target `Q in SO(n)`, `compile_so_on_tree` emits:

- a spanning tree;
- a leaf order;
- exactly `n(n-1)/2` edge Givens gates;
- the reconstructed operator;
- orthogonality and determinant residuals;
- proof that every gate lies on the authored graph.

This is exact up to floating-point evaluation and has no iterative solver.

### 6.2 Closed-control compiler

A physical performance often requires the visible controls to return to their initial values. In the three-mode path graph, define one rectangular primitive

\[
C(a,b)
=
R_{01}(a)R_{12}(b)R_{01}(-a)R_{12}(-b).
\]

Each primitive:

- closes both scalar controls exactly;
- consists only of zero-work rotations;
- can have nonidentity monodromy.

The prototype composes three such loops, giving six scalar parameters and twelve Givens factors, then solves for a target `SO(3)` operator and refines quadratic cost subject to exact endpoint constraints.

Three named targets at 37, 103, and 171 degrees were reconstructed with endpoint errors between `2.17e-16` and `4.32e-14` radians. A survey of 40 random `SO(3)` targets had:

- zero failures above `1e-8` radians;
- median endpoint error `3.39e-15` radians;
- maximum endpoint error `2.85e-13` radians;
- median endpoint-Jacobian condition number `2.84`.

This is strong numerical evidence, not yet a theorem that three rectangular commutators cover all of `SO(3)` under the chosen angle bounds.

### 6.3 Why this is not Solovay–Kitaev

The controls are continuous rotations, not a fixed finite gate alphabet. Exact tree QR is therefore stronger than approximate finite-alphabet synthesis for open paths. A Solovay–Kitaev-style theorem becomes relevant only after the implementation quantizes allowed angles, presets, or loop motifs. The immediate closed-loop problem is instead a constrained sub-Riemannian synthesis problem.

### 6.4 General compiler objective

A practical compiler minimizes a lexicographic or weighted objective such as

\[
\begin{aligned}
J(\gamma)={}&
\alpha\,d(U_\gamma,U_*)^2
+\beta\,d(H_\gamma,H_*)^2\\
&+\eta\,D[\gamma]
+\rho\,\operatorname{Var}(W_\gamma)
+\kappa\,N_{\mathrm{factors}}
+\xi\,A_{\mathrm{artifact}}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Hard constraints include:

- closed visible controls;
- duration;
- work and cumulative-deformation budgets;
- loss and radiation bounds;
- graph-edge availability;
- actuator velocity and acceleration;
- numerical ledger closure;
- robustness to parameter and timing perturbations.

The output is a factor schedule plus a receipt, not merely an optimizer vector.

## 7. Topological summit: two ingredients, not yet their fusion

The phrase “topologically protected instrument” is earned only if a declared output is invariant under a class of gap-preserving homotopies. Two different prototypes clarify the remaining work.

### 7.1 Abelian protected pump

The reference Rice–Mele cycle has a lower-band Chern number

\[
C=1.0000000000000007,
\]

minimum sampled gap `1.208`, and polarization winding exactly `1.0`. A topologically trivial offset loop gave Chern number `2.05e-17`. Sixteen random smooth, gap-preserving time warps retained the nontrivial invariant with maximum error `2.66e-15`.

The complex Schrödinger generator realifies as

\[
\mathcal A(H)=
\begin{bmatrix}
\operatorname{Im}H & \operatorname{Re}H\\
-\operatorname{Re}H & \operatorname{Im}H
\end{bmatrix},
\]

which is real skew-symmetric for Hermitian `H` and therefore preserves Euclidean acoustic energy. One four-dimensional propagator step compiled exactly to six path-edge Givens gates with reconstruction error `1.56e-16`.

This is a software-network topological pump seed. It is not yet an Ariadne delay-network timbre, a listener result, or a non-Abelian braid.

### 7.2 Non-Abelian constant-gap bundle

A separate four-complex-dimensional model uses a rank-two projector

\[
P(\lambda)=V(\lambda)V(\lambda)^\dagger
\]

and Hamiltonian

\[
H(\lambda)=I-2P(\lambda).
\]

It has a doubly degenerate lower subspace and an exactly constant gap of two. Two rectangular loops in different parameter planes produced Wilson operators with eigenphases

\[
\pm0.62385,\qquad \pm0.57814
\]

and noncommutator norm

\[
\|W_AW_B-W_BW_A\|_F=0.9029.
\]

Trace and determinant were invariant under a smooth changing frame gauge to about `2.4e-14`. The realified eight-dimensional instantaneous propagator compiled into 28 path-edge Givens gates with error `4.59e-16`.

However, a smooth deformation within the same ordinary homotopy class changed the Wilson matrix by `8.08e-3` Frobenius norm. This is deliberately recorded as a negative result: the prototype is **non-Abelian and geometric, but not topologically protected**.

### 7.3 The actual summit experiment

A protected non-Abelian Ariadne requires all of the following:

1. a degenerate acoustic mode subspace separated by a maintained spectral gap;
2. admissible parameter loops with a braid or non-Abelian pump invariant;
3. zero or explicitly budgeted work under the Ariadne ledger;
4. a compiler from the protected class to sparse delay/scattering factors;
5. robustness under declared timing, path-shape, and component perturbations;
6. an audible or controllable difference associated with the protected output.

The Rice–Mele result supplies protection without non-Abelian transport. The Grassmann bundle supplies non-Abelian transport without protection. Fusing them is the honest next research problem.

The Lean module `formal/MusFormal/Braid.lean` proves an exact finite signed-axis witness: adjacent quarter-turn Givens operations do not commute but satisfy `ABA=BAB`. It is a braid-relation representation with additional finite-order relations, not a topological invariant.

## 8. Passivity as type soundness

The compiler should reject unaccounted energy in the same way a typed language rejects an invalid program.

A configuration-changing operation has an effect type containing:

```text
before metric and rank
after metric and rank
source work
control work
internal loss
radiation/output
retired state
numerical tolerance
```

Composition is legal only when the state/metric interfaces match and every removed state subspace has one handler:

- radiate;
- dissipate;
- retire into a decaying tail;
- reject.

There is no “drop” effect.

The pure-core Lean module `formal/MusFormal/PassivityTypes.lean` uses natural-number energy quanta to prove:

\[
E_{\mathrm{after}}+E_{\mathrm{disposed}}
=
E_{\mathrm{before}}+E_{\mathrm{supplied}}
\]

for every well-typed program, and proves that an unforced program cannot increase the account. It is a finite skeleton, not the final SPD-metric theorem.

The production soundness target is:

> If a MUS-F acoustic program type-checks and its compiled primitives satisfy their local metric contracts, then every render has a complete energy receipt; no configuration change can inject or discard unaccounted energy outside the declared numerical tolerance.

The next formal layer needs mathlib support for finite-dimensional inner-product spaces, SPD square roots, rectangular polar decomposition, and partial isometries.

## 9. What appears genuinely new versus adapted

### Established mathematics or neighboring work

- connected edge rotations generating orthogonal transformations;
- restricted Givens QR;
- Lie-algebra controllability tests;
- polar and Cartan decompositions;
- thermodynamic length and minimum-dissipation protocols;
- holonomic quantum computation;
- Rice–Mele and Thouless pumps;
- non-Abelian acoustic braiding in physical structures;
- linear resource and effect type systems.

### Candidate Ariadne contribution

The plausible integrated contribution is:

> A typed inverse compiler for authored, time-varying acoustic graphs that decides operator reachability, synthesizes sparse control paths, minimizes a physically declared gesture metric, emits exact work and monodromy receipts, and supports protected mode-transport classes without treating the room as a post-effect.

The space remains part of the same graph. Its long-delay modes and boundary controls simply enlarge `n`, the edge topology, the metric, the friction tensor, and the admissible actuator set. The exact reachability theorem therefore applies across string, body, and room coordinates whenever their combined zero-work coupling graph is connected.

## 10. Research stop conditions

The next paper is not earned by adding more notation. It is earned when the following are true in the production system:

1. `SO(n)` target compilation lowers into the Rust factor executor and reproduces the oracle.
2. Closed controls compile to same-endpoint audible Weave/space gestures.
3. A measured friction metric predicts at least one perceptual smoothness or artifact ranking out of sample.
4. The deformation budget closes on real variable-delay and topology-change operations.
5. The instrument–body–room graph is bidirectional and shares one metric ledger.
6. At least one protected pump survives declared perturbations after lowering to the acoustic graph.
7. The typechecker rejects intentionally malformed energy and rank-change programs.
8. Lean proves the real-valued local soundness theorem used by the compiler.

Until then, the exact results in this packet are foundations and executable hypotheses, not a claim that the summit has been reached.
